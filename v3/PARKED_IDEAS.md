# V3 — Parked Ideas

Status: unvetted brainstorm, captured 2026-09-02 so they aren't lost.
**None of these are decisions** — they're things worth reconsidering once the
core loop (Champion / Tardigrade / Energy / Affiliation, see
`DESIGN_NOTES.md`) is further along, not commitments made against it.

1. **Market / deckbuilding purchase track.** On your turn, spend energy
   either on fielding a character OR on buying a card from a shared Market
   that grants your team an ability. Plays a similar role to a Basic Action
   die — could be additive, or could replace it outright.

2. **Non-d6 dice for high-variance effects.** Digital removes the
   physical-manufacturing constraint, so nothing requires every die to be a
   d6. One concrete idea: a high-cost d20 that deals damage equal to
   whatever it rolls, then returns to its card — a genuine push-your-luck
   swing card. (This came from a kid who likes going all-in on long shots —
   expect more push-your-luck suggestions from that direction.)

3. **Worker-placement element.** Instead of fielding a die into the Field
   Zone, send it to a "Location" card for a bonus effect. The die is out of
   play until it returns.

4. **Sabotage via bad dice.** An ability that gives an opponent a bad
   Tardigrade — single-energy-face or even blank-face only. Could be a
   straight replacement of one of their dice, or additive (clogging their
   bag, slowing their cycle) — if additive, the opponent might get to pay X
   energy to remove it.

5. **Culling your own Tardigrades.** A way to permanently remove Tardigrades
   from your own circulation, thinning your own bag. Noted as already
   partially achieved by fielding them — may be redundant with #4's removal
   side, or may want its own distinct cost/effect to be worth doing.

6. **Debt die.** A free "purchase": immediately grants 2 energy and
   auto-fields a die you cannot otherwise remove. While it's in the Field
   Zone, you can't purchase dice starting your following turn. Pay 3 to
   return it to its card.

7. **Gambler die.** A free purchase, rolled immediately on acquisition, with
   a mixed table of good and bad effects.

8. **"Unblocked bonus" evasion ability** (2026-10-03, from the Infiltrate
   design talk). When unblocked, the creature deals its normal damage AND
   draws a die into your Prep Area (Ricochet's follow-up folded in).
   Easy to price, rewards evasive attackers. The user liked it for some
   future character; Infiltrate itself kept the Dice Masters rule.

9. **"Can't be blocked by anything bigger"** (2026-10-03, same talk). A
   sneaky-small-animal evasion keyword: can't be blocked by creatures
   whose DEF is higher than its ATK. Also earmarked for a future
   character.

10. **Several cards per animal, one per team** (2026-10-12, user, while
    weighing the pack system's all-unique animals). Dice Masters printed
    a character several times (common/uncommon/rare Black Widow), each a
    unique, separately tracked card (Superman SKC133 vs SKC149) with the
    same title and die. A team could hold only one card of a given name,
    so you chose which Black Widow - which let two strong abilities sit on
    one character without ever being played together. Open question,
    pending player feedback: Dice Kingdom has all the world's animals, so
    it isn't short of characters the way a licensed set was - all-unique
    may be fine. If adopted: card ids stay unique (collections and
    rarities already key on them), add a one-card-per-name team rule, and
    decide whether a die belongs to the card or is shared by the name.

## Tension flagged alongside these

If it gets easier to field chump blockers — and the Tardigrade L1/L2 faces
already lean that way (0/1 and 1/1 bodies) — board states risk stalling into
a "walled up" stalemate. Worth weighing against any idea here that adds more
ways to generate cheap bodies (Market, worker-placement, debt/gambler dice
all push in that direction to some degree).
